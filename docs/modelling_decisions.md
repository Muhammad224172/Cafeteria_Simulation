# Modelling decisions

The problem statement leaves several points open. Where the group's own Arena model (Part 2) or
the instructor's solution model (Part 3) made a readable choice, I note it. Those choices were
read from text strings in the compiled Arena files, since the `.doe`/`.p` files are binary. Each
choice is a named switch in `configs/base.yaml`. Where a choice moves the results noticeably, a
scenario in `configs/scenarios/` measures by how much (results in `results/baseline/scenarios.csv`).

Sources are abbreviated as **Spec** (the assignment), **Group** (the group's Part 2 Arena model)
and **Instructor** (the instructor's solution model).

## Arrivals

**1. Is the rate table in customers or in arrival events?** The table's column header says
customers per hour, but the text describes counts of arrivals, and each arrival brings a group of
1 to 6 people (mean 1.50).
*Choice: arrival events per hour, so each event brings a group* (`arrivals.rate_unit: groups`).
This gives about 3,340 customers a day.
- *Instructor:* creates one entity per event from the schedule 600/450/180/150/450/240 per hour,
  then duplicates it into the group. This is the same reading.
- *Group:* also treats the schedule values as arrival events of whole groups, but entered them at
  a third of the table (200/150/60/50/150/80 per hour).
- *Other reading* (scenario `s01_rate_in_customers`): about 2,240 customers a day. Measure 1
  falls from 47.6 s to 41.7 s, and measure 2 falls from 491 to 164, because the dining hall is no
  longer saturated.

**2. Generating the arrival process.** Arrivals are generated by thinning (Lewis-Shedler) against
the peak rate, so the rate steps exactly at the period boundaries. Group size is drawn per event.

## Routing

**3. Do group members choose lines individually?** The Spec quotes the 10/60/30 split per
customer and says a group "may dissolve" while getting food.
*Choice: each member draws their own meal type* (`routing.meal_choice: per_customer`).
- The group's standard eaters go to the same B/D line, chosen when the group arrives. Otherwise
  the shortest-line rule would split two friends with the same meal between B and D, which is
  not what people do.
- *Group:* draws the line per member after duplicating the group.
- *Instructor:* draws one line for the whole group (`per_group`, used in scenario `s02`). That
  setting adds about 3.5 s to measure 1, because groups arrive at a station in bursts.

**4. What does "shorter queue" mean for lines B and D?** *Choice: the number of customers who have
joined the line and not yet finished paying, with ties going to B.* This is the Instructor's
variable (`customers in line`). The other candidate, the queue at the tray alone, is nearly always 0 or 1
people, so it would carry little information.

## Serving lines

**5. Station capacity and blocking.** Each station (tray, the four food stations, cutlery) holds
one customer.
- A customer keeps their station while picking an item and while walking to the next one, then
  joins the next station's queue. This matches the Instructor.
- Customers who skip an item still pass through the station.
- I include all six walks (tray to dessert through cutlery to payment). The Instructor model has
  no walk between tray and dessert, so it has about 1.5 s less of this.

**6. Empty shelf.** A customer who wants an item that is not on the shelf waits at the station,
still occupying it, until a worker puts one there (Instructor: the same).

**7. Cutlery.** Each customer takes one set, and a line holds 100 sets. The Spec mentions 100
forks, 100 knives and 100 spoons; I treat them as 100 sets, as the Instructor does.

**8. Payment.** There is one till per line with a FIFO queue. The payment type and the
enough-credit draw are made per customer, and the top-up time includes the payment.

**9. After payment.** Only customers who visit the sauce table walk UNIF(5, 15) s to it. The
walk to the dining hall is not modelled, since the Spec gives no time for it. A group sits down
once its last member has paid and finished at the sauce table.

## Workers and tasks

**10. Task 3 plating time (main, side, soup).** The assignment's data file
(`Task3_MainSideSoup_times.csv`) is not in the course folder. The instructor's Part 1(c) script
fits a normal distribution to it by maximum likelihood, but the script's output is not in the
folder either.
- I checked the four `Data_Input_Analysis_*.txt` files in the course folder. They are input-analysis
  samples (n = 60, mean about 119; n = 50, roughly uniform on 0-40; n = 250, roughly uniform on
  20-70; n = 250, exponential-looking). None is labelled as the Task 3 data, and none is on the
  scale of a few seconds per plate.
- The Instructor model's expressions name the distribution explicitly:
  `main side soup serving time = NORM(8, 2)` seconds.
- *Choice: NORM(8, 2) s, truncated at 0* (`supplies.prepared_item_time`). The truncation matters
  only with probability below 1e-4. If the data file turns up, refit and change the two numbers.

**11. Task priorities.** *Choice: strict Task 1 > Task 2 > Task 3, as the Spec says.* Within a
priority, the condition that has been pending longest goes first. The Instructor model gives
Tasks 1 and 2 the same seize priority, which matters little because both are rare (about 15 to 20
cutlery refills a day per area).

**12. How a task is defined.** Tasks are conditions on the supply state, not queued jobs.
- One Task 3 places one item. The worker then chooses again, so an emptied container (Task 1)
  pre-empts the remaining top-ups at the next item boundary. Tasks themselves are never
  interrupted.
- A top-up takes its unit out of the container when it starts. The last unit therefore empties
  the container, and the replacement task becomes pending at that moment.

**13. How many workers can top up the same shelf?** *Choice: one at a time per shelf section*
(`workers.max_workers_per_shelf: 1`).
- The space behind one section is small.
- The Instructor model has one top-up entity per shelf section, which gives the same limit.
- Without the limit (scenario `s03`), stockout waiting drops from 0.42 to 0.25 s per customer and
  measure 1 from 47.6 to 46.8 s.

**14. Which idle worker takes a task?** *Choice: the one idle longest* (`workers.dispatch:
longest_idle`). The Instructor model picks randomly between the experienced and inexperienced
pools. Preferring experienced workers is one of the alternatives tested (`a01`); it makes no
measurable difference.

**15. Inexperienced workers.** Each sampled task time is multiplied by 1.4, so the mean and the
spread both scale. A test checks the factor is exactly 1.4.
- In the copy of the Instructor model on disk, the slowdown expression is
  `(0.4*(staff index <= 4) + 1)` over a set of two resources. That makes it 1.4 for *both* pools,
  which looks like an artefact of later edits.
- I did not reproduce it. With every task time multiplied by 1.4, measure 1 under the Instructor
  reading rises to about 74 s, far from the Instructor's 54 s, so the base run it produced
  probably did not have it.

**16. Shift changes.** Staffing changes at each schedule breakpoint. A worker whose shift ends
finishes the task in hand and then leaves, which is like Arena's "Wait" schedule rule. The last
staffing level (from 19:00) applies until the day ends, which the Spec states as "and after".

## Dining

**17. Group dining time.** The Spec says each person eats for UNIF(15, 30) minutes and the group
leaves when the last member finishes. The assignment's own Part 1(b) answer derives
`15*BETA(k, 1) + 15` for that maximum, in Arena's BETA(beta, alpha) parameter order.
- The Instructor model's dining delay is written `15*BETA(1, group size) + 15`, with the arguments
  swapped. In Arena's parameterisation that is the distribution of the *first* member to finish
  (mean 15 + 15/(k+1) minutes) rather than the last (mean 15 + 15k/(k+1)).
- *Choice: the last member* (`dining.group_eating: last_member`), as the problem says.
- `first_member` is available for validation. It is the main reason measure 2 differs from the
  Instructor model: see `validation.md`.

**18. Table choice.** A party takes an empty table if there is one (lowest number on ties).
Otherwise it takes the least occupied table that can seat it. This is the same as Arena's
"largest remaining capacity" selection used by the Instructor. A test covers the cases.

**19. Table queue discipline.** *Choice: strict FIFO*, so a party of 5 at the head of the queue is
not bypassed by a single who would fit (`dining.allow_overtaking: false`). Allowing overtaking
(scenario `s04`) cuts the mean table wait from 59 to 33 minutes and measure 2 from 491 to 408,
because singles fill gaps. The Spec does not say which; FIFO is the conservative reading.

## Day boundaries and statistics

**20. Initial state.** At 11:30 all shelves, containers and cutlery are full, and the first
period's staff are on duty. There is no warm-up: the system is terminating and starts empty each
day.

**21. When the day ends, and the window for measure 2.** The Spec closes the cafeteria when it is
past 20:00 and no one is in a serving line. A serving line here means arrival to end of payment.
- I simulate until the last diner leaves anyway, so customer conservation can be checked and
  every group's dining time is recorded.
- Measure 1 is a tally over every customer. All customers have paid by the closing time, so it is
  unaffected by the choice.
- Measure 2 is the time-weighted average of the number of customers in the cafeteria over
  `[11:30, T]`. By default T is the Spec's closing time (`day.measure2_window: lines_empty`,
  within a minute of 20:00 on average).
- The Instructor model stops when it is past 20:00 *and the whole cafeteria is empty*, about 21:07
  on average there. Averaging until the last diner leaves (`system_empty`) is also reported, and
  is the figure used for validation. It is lower, because the last hour is a draining tail.

**22. Labour cost.** Cost is deterministic given the schedule, in units of one inexperienced
worker-hour.
- Base cost: 2 areas x 21.5 h x (1.3 + 1.0) = 98.90 for workers, plus 4 cashiers x 8.5 h x 1.3 =
  44.20, a total of **143.10** per day.
- Hours after 20:00 are unpaid.
- A closed line's cashier is unpaid while the line is closed (`costs.cashier_paid_when_line_closed`).
  Otherwise closing a line could never save money, and the assignment suggests closing B or D as
  a cost lever.

## Levers added for the alternatives

**23. Closing line B or D.** During a closure, new standard customers go to the other line.
Customers already in the closed line finish normally. Lines A and C cannot close, since they are
the only vegan and salad lines.

**24. A second cashier.** The assignment's list of example levers ends with "etc.", and the
group's own Part 3 file used two-unit cashiers. The base results also show that the till is the
largest single source of queueing in the lines, while workers are idle 70% of the time. So I
allow a scheduled second till at a line, sharing one FIFO queue, paid like a cashier. When the
second cashier goes off duty, they finish the customer in hand.

## Random numbers

**25. Streams and common random numbers.** Thirteen named streams per replication, seeded from
`SeedSequence(seed, spawn_key=(replication, stream))`. The streams cover arrivals, group size,
meal, item wanted, item time, walk, tray, cutlery, payment, sauce, eating, worker tasks and
dispatch.
- Every random attribute of a customer is drawn at arrival, in arrival order, from its own
  stream. A fixed number of draws is made per customer, whether or not the attribute is used.
- So for a given replication index, every configuration sees the same customers with the same
  needs and times. Tests check this.
- Only the worker task times, whose order depends on the schedule, are not perfectly synchronised.
